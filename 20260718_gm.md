# FORGE: Forward-Only RLVR with GRZO Estimators
## Group Meeting Summary — 2026-07-18
(Data as of 07-17. † = internal protocol (max_tokens=1024); exact-paper-protocol (512) reruns in progress.)

---

## 1. Problem & Motivation

**Goal**: Run RLVR fine-tuning (the GRPO family) **without backpropagation** — no stored
activations, no gradients, no optimizer states. If a machine can *serve* the model, it can
*train* it.

**Idea**: Keep GRPO's rollout, verifier reward, and group-relative advantage exactly.
Replace only the backprop update with a zeroth-order estimator.

**Lineage — the synthesis of our two prior papers**:
- **GRZO** (our LLM ZO optimizer): per-example perturbations, group-relative normalization;
- **TCAD yield optimization** (our circuit ZO work): smooth margin surrogate, Spearman
  calibration gate, homotopy.

Both fields solve the same mathematical object: **ZO optimization of a stochastic
indicator objective** (pass/fail yield ↔ 0/1 verifiable reward), which is piecewise
constant with zero gradient almost everywhere.

---

## 2. Method: Three-Layer Construction

```
Layer 0  True objective (scoring only, never trained on):  J(θ) = E[1{answer correct}]
Layer 1  Margin smoothing (from TCAD):    r = exp(−relative_miss/τ),
                                          τ homotopy 0.2 → 0.02 (anneals back to binary)
Layer 2  Group advantage (from GRPO):     Â = (r − group mean)/group std,  G = 8 per prompt
Layer 3  Surrogate + GRZO estimator:      L(θ) = −Σ Â · log π_θ(y|x)/|y|
                                          δ_j = L(θ+σε_j) − L(θ−σε_j)  (two-point, CRN)
                                          θ ← θ − (lr/N) Σ c_j ε_j
Guard    Spearman(margin, binary) logged at every eval — the calibration gate.
```

**Why this is principled**: at the sampling point, ∇L equals the true policy gradient
(REINFORCE identity) — the surrogate is gradient-exact, not an approximation. ZO adds
only the O(σ²) smoothing bias (same analysis skeleton as the TCAD paper).

**The efficiency asymmetry that drives everything**: evaluating a perturbation requires
only *re-reading* existing answers (one parallel forward), not *writing* new ones.
FORGE writes **64 answers per step**; ES writes **6,000** (30 perturbations × 200
problems each). Widening FORGE's averaging is nearly free; widening ES's costs full
generations.

## 3. Main Results

### 3.1 Efficiency: FORGE dominates the cost axis †

**Rollouts per training step (the dominant cost in RLVR):**

| | rollouts/step | relative |
|---|---|---|
| ES (paper recipe) | 30 × 200 = **6,000** | 94× |
| GRPO (typical) | ~8–256 | 1–4× |
| **FORGE** | **64** | **1×** |

**Budget to reach 3% accuracy on Countdown (base 1.85%):**

| method | generations needed |
|---|---|
| **FORGE + margin** | **5,600** |
| FORGE (binary reward) | ~120,000 |
| ES | ~30,000+ |

FORGE+margin is **20× cheaper than our own binary variant and ~6× cheaper than ES** to
first meaningful gains; in the low-budget regime (<10k generations) nothing else moves at all.

### 3.2 Countdown (Qwen2.5-1.5B, base 1.85%, 2000-problem eval) †

| method | best accuracy | notes |
|---|---|---|
| v1 (ZO on raw 0/1, greedy) | ≤ base | **Falsified**: piecewise-constant objective — the yield pathology |
| **FORGE (500 steps)** | **9.7% (5.2×)** | Reproduced across 2 recipes × 2 seeds; still rising at stop |
| FORGE + margin + τ-anneal | stable plateau, no collapse | Homotopy cures the late-run instability seen in all other configs |
| ES (150 steps, 900k gens) | 21.9% | Reaches higher absolute accuracy — by brute-force averaging at 94× rollout cost/step; equal-budget convergence duel queued |

### 3.3 MATH500 (Qwen2.5-1.5B, base 54.0%) — where ES breaks down †

| method | trajectory |
|---|---|
| ES (150 steps, **900k** generations) | **No trend** (base ±3pp noise) |
| **FORGE margin-tiebreak** (~40k generations) | Consistently above binary variant (~+2pp); 10 of 12 evals ≥ base; calibration gate **0.95+** |

ES's large-batch averaging only pays when mining sparse signal. On dense-signal tasks
(base 54%), it burns 900k generations for nothing; FORGE is the only method here with a
consistent positive trajectory — at 1/20 the cost.

### 3.4 The spectrum finding

Averaging width m per perturbation direction governs sparse-task performance
(m = 1 dies → surrogate revives it → m = 8 doubles speed → m = 200 is ES).
FORGE widens m at *re-read* cost; ES widens m at *generation* cost. This asymmetry is
structural and is the source of the low-budget dominance.

### 3.5 The calibration gate at work

With pure margin reward at scale, training margin rose while true accuracy fell and the
Spearman gate slid 0.49 → 0.31: **reward hacking caught live** (the model learned
"close" instead of "exact"). The run was terminated by the gate criterion and the reward
recalibrated (tiebreak / τ-annealed variants). This is the TCAD calibration discipline
transferring across domains — a design element, not an accident.

## 4. ES-at-Scale Paper Replication (validating our baseline)

Paper's exact recipe (pop 30, σ=1e-3, α=5e-4, batch 200) **at the paper's own budget**
(Fig. 7: 3M sample evaluations = 500 iterations), 7 models, our automated relay
infrastructure:

| model | paper ES (3M rollouts) | ours (exact protocol, same budget*) |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen-0.5B | 14.4% | 13.75% (95%) |
| Llama-1B | 16.8% | 12.75% (76%) |
| **Qwen-1.5B** | 37.3% | **37.9% (102%)** |
| Qwen-3B | 60.5% | 50.2% (83%, 80% budget) |
| Qwen-7B | 66.8% | 52.5% (78%, 60% budget) |
| Llama-3B / 8B | 51.6% / 61.2% | in flight |

\*0.5B/1B/1.5B at the paper's full 3M-rollout budget; 3B at 80%, 7B/8B at 60%.
